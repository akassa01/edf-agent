## 1. mcnamara-vietnam-body-count
`scientist` · `international` · `documented` · historical (1961–68) · Asia

- **Dominant mode:** thinking. **Crowded out:** motive, history, identity.
- **Trusted instrument:** body count as a proxy for winning. Internally consistent,
  externally meaningless.
- **1+1:** the US was answering "are we killing more of them than they are of us?" The
  Vietnamese were answering "how long can we endure?" Two separate ones.
- **Where is X:** the moment the metric was chosen. Everything downstream optimized faithfully
  toward the wrong target.
- **18th camel:** ask how the other side defines winning, and staff the room with people who
  could answer — regional historians, Vietnamese political analysts.
- **Decision pathway:** Reframe.
- **Flag:** ego. The US entered with a great deal of it, and principled pragmatism does not
  address ego.

## 2. challenger-oring-launch
`scientist–engineer` · `individual` · `documented` · historical (1986) · Americas

- **Dominant mode:** thinking — specifically the engineer-to-engineer evidentiary mode: here
  is the data, here is the conclusion, the conversation is over.
- **Crowded out:** institutions and standing. Who in that room could actually halt a launch.
- **Where is X:** the teleconference, Jan 27 1986. Boisjoly was right and in the wrong
  language for the room.
- **18th camel:** reframe O-ring risk as institutional liability rather than engineering
  probability; identify who had standing to stop the launch and speak to them; escalate
  outside the teleconference.
- **Decision pathway:** Reframe.
- **Flag:** raw organizational power structure. Principled pragmatism cannot dissolve a
  hierarchy that has already decided.

## 3. emma-daniel-workplace-concern
`humanist` · `individual` · **composite** · contemporary · unspecified

- **Dominant mode:** feeling, plus a binary framing (intervene vs. respect privacy) that was
  itself the source of the dilemma.
- **Crowded out:** actionability. Emma had urgency and no authority, no diagnosis, no
  certainty.
- **Urgency–actionability trap:** the textbook instance. The more urgent it felt, the less
  obvious the right response became.
- **Solomon check:** explicit and central. She knew exactly what to tell a friend in the same
  situation. "Emma was struck by how much easier the problem seemed when it belonged to
  someone else."
- **18th camel:** stop choosing between control and passivity. Create opportunities for
  conversation; widen the network so support can emerge from coworkers, friends, family. It
  guarantees nothing — it changes the structure of the problem.
- **Decision pathway:** Reframe.
- **Flag:** the case never resolves. Principled pragmatism gives you an actionable space, not
  an outcome — and for a person who is frightened for someone they care about, that may not
  be enough.
- **Note:** an illustrative composite, not a documented episode. Labeled as such.

## 4. hawaii-false-missile-alert
`engineer` · `community` · `documented` · recent (2018) · Americas

- **Dominant mode:** doing — a system optimized for speed. **Crowded out:** recovery, and the
  felt experience of fear.
- **Diagnosis:** the system was built to send alerts, not to recover from them. Thinking
  (speed-optimized design, no confirmation step), feeling (trust in the system, no guidance
  beyond "seek shelter", 38 minutes of imagined worst cases), and doing (no protocol for
  reversal, fragmented authority) each behaved as designed. The misalignment was the failure.
- **Where is X:** unusually clean — the 38 minutes between *knowing the alert was false* and
  *being able to say so.* Not the mistaken activation.
- **18th camel:** the framing was speed vs. accuracy. Pre-scripted correction messages,
  dedicated cancellation protocols, distributed authority, and simulation exercises don't
  balance the two — they improve both. A redesign, not a compromise.
- **Decision pathway:** Redesign.
- **Flag:** the durable harm is to trust in the warning system itself. If the next alert is
  disbelieved, the cost of this failure is paid in a future emergency — and no framework
  gives you a way to rebuild that credibility on demand.

## 5. raj-solomon-visa-decision
`humanist` · `individual` · **composite** · recent · Americas / Asia

- **Dominant mode:** feeling — but the striking part is that it wasn't Raj's *only* mode. He
  reasoned pragmatically and well for Farhan, and could not do it for himself.
- **Crowded out:** the pragmatism he had just demonstrated. Feeling stopped being a data point
  and became the whole analysis.
- **Where is X:** the moment he stops reasoning as an advisor and starts reasoning as the
  person affected.
- **18th camel:** for Farhan, Raj found it — the decision isn't US-vs-India; a US education
  opens Europe and the Middle East. For himself he never looked.
- **Solomon check:** the entire case. Kross & Grossmann: self-distancing via third-person
  framing is the countermeasure.
- **Decision pathway:** Reframe.
- **Flag:** knowing about the Solomon Paradox does not exempt you from it. Raj could have
  named the bias and still fallen into it — and no framework tells you how to notice, from
  the inside, that you are currently inside it.
- **Note:** illustrative composite. Labeled.

## 6. inderlok-dmrc-terminus
`engineer` · `community` · `documented` · recent (Phase 4 planning) · Asia

- **Dominant mode:** doing — optimize the interchange that exists, for the commuters who are
  here now. **Crowded out:** long-horizon thinking about extensibility.
- **1+1:** the Green Line engineers answered "what do we need now?" The Phase 4 planners
  answered "where does the city need to go?" The parties never agreed which question they
  were jointly solving, so 1+1 produced two separate ones.
- **Where is X:** the best in the corpus. A single dead-end platform at a single station is
  the fulcrum on which an entire Phase 4 corridor turns. Fix Inderlok, unlock 4–6 km of
  unserved Central Delhi.
- **18th camel:** the binary was (A) don't extend, or (B) build a whole new line. The third
  thing: a new *underground* station at Inderlok for through-running service, retaining the
  elevated platforms for terminating trains. More expensive than an extension, cheaper than a
  line, more coherent than either.
- **Decision pathway:** Redesign.
- **Solomon check:** DMRC's planners would plausibly have flagged the terminal geometry as a
  future risk in *another city's* network. The wisdom was available; it just wasn't directed
  inward. Expertise breeds proximity, and proximity narrows vision.
- **Flag:** the humanist's question has no good answer — who pays for the fix? Fare hikes,
  construction disruption, years of delayed coverage. Principled pragmatism identifies the
  18th camel but does not tell you how to distribute its cost.
