# STYLE.md — Engineering Diplomacy case studies (v2)

## The frame

Written in the intellectual frame of Prof. Shafiqul Islam: the interplay between
**numbers and narratives, principles and pragmatism, facts and values.**

Each case examines a real (or explicitly labeled illustrative) decision made under
uncertainty, where the facts were contested *and* the values were contested, and where one
mode of reasoning was over-weighted at the expense of the others. The lesson is
**engineering diplomacy**: technical correctness is necessary but not sufficient, and so is
empathy, and so is executability. You need all three, and the failure is almost always one
crowding out the rest.

**A response worth defending is scientifically defensible, socially acceptable, and
politically feasible.** Drop any one and the pathway dies.

---

## The three archetypes

Every case is read from all three seats before it is written — even when one dominates the
final prose. (These are the workshop's Session-2 panel under different names: Scientific,
Social, Institutional.)

| Archetype | Mode | Asks |
|---|---|---|
| **Scientist** | Thinking | What do we know, and how well? What is measurable? |
| **Humanist** | Feeling | Who is affected? What is contested, and what does it mean to them? |
| **Engineer** | Doing | What is buildable given real constraints? Who can act, coordinate, or block? |

The seed corpus deliberately spans all three (see `SEED_CASES.md`). Do not let the corpus
drift back toward scientist-archetype failure stories.

## The canon — use this vocabulary, not invented substitutes

- **What is 1+1?** — the answer depends on what is being added and on the assumptions behind
  the question. When parties never agree on which question they are jointly answering, 1+1
  produces two separate ones rather than 2. Shared understanding is not assumed; it is
  constructed.
- **Where is X?** — where is the intervention point? The most leveraged interventions in
  complex systems are rarely the largest; they are the most precisely located.
- **What is the 18th camel?** — the option outside the apparent binary. *Not* a compromise
  between the two options on the table: a third thing that changes the structure of the
  problem. (House standard is **18th**, per Islam & Susskind. Some drafts say "17th" — do
  not follow them.)
- **The Solomon Paradox** — we reason more wisely about others' problems than our own,
  because distance enables perspective that proximity forecloses. Self-distancing is the
  countermeasure. It applies to institutions as much as to people.
- **The urgency–actionability trap** — urgency does not produce clarity. It escalates faster
  than the capacity to act meaningfully, and the gap between them is where damage happens.
- **Joint fact-finding**, adaptive experiments over final solutions, acting under
  uncertainty and revising as new facts emerge.

## The structure (follow the rhythm; do NOT print section labels)

1. **Title** — "The [Name] [Fallacy / Disaster / Paradox]" or the episode's name.
2. **Cold open** — a specific person at a specific decisive moment, with date and place.
   (For systemic cases: a specific person *inside* the system, at the moment it broke.)
3. **The setup** — their competence and their method; the mode they leaned on.
4. **The failure** — what the dominant mode had no room for, and why that dimension was
   causally decisive. Frame it epistemologically where apt: precision vs. accuracy, numbers
   vs. narratives, facts vs. values, speed vs. recovery.
5. **The three readings** — what the Scientist, the Humanist, and the Engineer each see
   here. Woven into the prose, not bulleted. Each should see something the others miss.
6. **The metaphors** — locate X. Ask what 1+1 produced. Name the 18th camel, or defend its
   absence.
7. **The pathway** — concretely, what a principled pragmatist could have done: one clear
   move, and who had the standing to make it.
8. **The flag** — close with a parenthetical, honest limitation: something the episode
   reveals that principled pragmatism itself does **not** resolve.
9. **The synthesis block** — the workshop's seven components (below).
10. **References** — 3–6 real, verifiable sources (`documented` cases only).

## The synthesis block

Every case closes with this, in the workshop's own format. It is what makes the corpus
usable as BUET anchor-problem material rather than a pile of essays:

> **Problem** — the decision problem in one sentence
> **Stakeholders** — who matters and why
> **Binding constraint** — what actually prevents action
> **Decision pathway** — Implement / Redesign / Expand / Reframe (pick exactly one)
> **Tools** — which, and how each changes interaction, learning, or coordination
> **Metrics** — measurable, trackable, linked to the pathway
> **90-day commitment** — who does what next

Retrospective cases state the synthesis counterfactually: the pathway that *was available*
at the decisive moment. Prospective cases state it as a live recommendation.

## Tone and length

Narrative, precise, unsentimental. Relatable and generalizable. **700–1000 words** of prose
per case, plus the synthesis block. No section headers inside the prose.
