## 1. The 16/16/16 balance lasted one cycle: thinking surges to 20

The 2026-08-11 digest reported the corpus's first perfectly balanced crowded-out-mode count:
feeling=16, doing=16, thinking=16. That balance lasted exactly one cycle. In six cases:

- gleick-heartland: humanist/thinking (feeling crowds out thinking)
- srebrenica: engineer/feeling (doing crowds out feeling) ← exception

- slotin: engineer/thinking (doing crowds out thinking)
- biafra: humanist/thinking (feeling crowds out thinking)

Four of six cases crowd out thinking. The updated distribution: **thinking=20, feeling=18,
doing=16.** Thinking is now the most-crowded-out mode by a margin of two cases, reversing
the perfect balance in a single run.

This is a finding about the selection process, not the population. The quota constraint (fill
the least-covered scale/archetype cell) generates individual/engineer and community/humanist
cases; the open slot selects for strong candidates regardless of mode. Neither constraint
directly selects for crowded-out mode. The surge in thinking-crowded-out is therefore
unmanaged and potentially informative: it may reflect that the strongest available cases
in the currently under-filled cells (international/humanist, community/humanist,
individual/engineer) tend to feature humanist and engineer archetypes crowding out thinking
— which is structurally coherent. The humanist who acts on feeling and forecloses analysis
(Gleick, Biafra's JCA, Samoa's anti-vax advocates) and the engineer who executes and
forecloses reflection (Slotin) are dominant failure archetypes precisely in cells the corpus
was trying to fill.

**Implication for upcoming runs:** doing remains the least-crowded-out mode (16 of 54,
30%). The last time doing was the least-covered, the quota incentive corrected for it
organically. The agent does not need to select for crowded-out mode — the quota mechanism
will produce the correction if the under-filled cells happen to contain doing-crowded-out
cases.

## 3. Reframe reaches 20; the two dominant pathways now pattern differently

Updated decision pathway counts (54 cases, two runs after the last digest):

| Pathway | Count | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Redesign | 24 | +2 (samoa, walkerton) |
| Reframe | 20 | +4 (gleick, srebrenica, slotin, biafra) |
| Expand | 4 | 0 |
| Implement | 2 | 0 |

Reframe grew faster this run (+4) than Redesign (+2). At 54 cases, the two dominant
pathways are now close enough (24 vs 20) to look at their structural differences:

**Redesign cases share:** a system whose architecture generates the wrong output even when
all actors behave as designed. The problem is in the geometry of the system, not in the
choices of individual actors. Walkerton: the notification architecture put a potential
deceiver in the sole notification role. Samoa: the immunization delivery architecture had
no mechanism for replacing lost trust before the epidemic curve arrived. The 18th camel
in Redesign cases is always an institutional form — a different feedback loop, a different
reporting chain, a different decision gate.

**Reframe cases share:** actors who are answering a question coherently but answering the
wrong question. The problem is in the definition of what is being solved. Gleick asked "how
do I prove Heartland is wrong?" rather than "what makes my scientific position
unassailable?". Srebrenica's Dutchbat asked "how do we maintain the safe area?" rather than
"what does our actual capability permit us to guarantee?". Slotin asked "how do we conduct
this critical assembly?" rather than "what does it mean that there is no margin between my
skill and catastrophe?". Biafra's JCA asked "how do we feed people under the blockade?"
rather than "is the blockade the problem we should be solving?" The 18th camel in Reframe
cases is always a question-substitution: replace the question being answered with a question
that would reveal the actual decision structure.

This structural distinction between Redesign and Reframe now holds across 44 of the 54 cases.
The ten exceptions are cases that straddle both pathways, which suggests those cases may be
somewhat weakly differentiated. Tracking for Prof. Islam's review.

## 4. Six new flags — and a new pattern: the funding-model lock

**gleick-heartland:** "fraud in service of a just cause damages the instrument without
touching the target." Category A variant: the actor's means undermine the actor's standing.
Not ego (seeking validation) and not organizational power (external hierarchy) — this is
self-inflicted standing loss. The Scientist's epistemic authority, which was the entire
basis for Gleick's credibility as a climate scientist, is precisely what he sacrificed. This
is the corpus's first flag about an actor destroying their own tool while trying to use it.

**srebrenica:** "presence without protection: a designation creates expectations it cannot
fulfill — and the designation itself may prevent the evacuation that could have saved lives."
Category D: institutional architecture creates a liability through its own terminology. This
is a distinct variant from previous Category D flags: not a gap in notification or
extradition — a positive institutional designation that creates harm precisely by being taken
seriously. The safe area designation was not a failure of execution; it was a structural
trap built into the concept.

**samoa:** "rational distrust is indistinguishable from irrational distrust in its
epidemiological effects; principled pragmatism cannot specify the timeline on which trust
is earned, nor guarantee that the timeline and the epidemic curve will not arrive in direct
conflict." Category E: the diagnostic-prescriptive gap. The framework can prescribe the
trust-building process; it cannot guarantee that trust and outbreak curve will not arrive
simultaneously. This is the first flag explicitly about the interaction between a governance
process timeline and a biophysical timeline — a temporal mismatch flag. The framework has no
tool for compressing the trust-building timeline without destroying the authenticity that
makes trust possible.

**slotin:** "expert authority and accountability cannot occupy the same body — when the
expert's judgment is the safety system, there is no safety system." Category D: institutional
architecture. Clean, fresh, maximally generalizable: applies to any system where the expert
who designs the test is also the sole test-taker. This is the strongest individual/engineer
flag in the corpus. It names a structural feature (expert authority + accountability fused)
rather than an individual failure (Slotin's recklessness). Compare to citicorp's flag:
"expert authority is not self-standing" — both are Category D/individual/engineer, but
citicorp's flag is about disclosure (authority must be socially confirmed), while slotin's
is about separation (authority and oversight cannot cohabit the same person).

**biafra:** "the humanitarian funding model makes the decisive political analysis financially
impossible." This is a genuinely new flag structure. Not Category D (institutional
architecture) in the traditional sense — the architecture is working as designed. Not
Category A (actor's limits) — the JCA was fully capable of performing the political
analysis. This is a **constitutive incentive lock**: the organization's financial survival
requires it to produce the operational output (feeding) that makes the analytical output
(blockade is the problem) impossible to publish. The instrument that makes the mission
viable also makes honest mission-evaluation impossible. Proposing as **Category J:
constitutive-incentive lock** — the institutional structure that funds or enables the actor
is the same structure that makes the actor's self-critical analysis financially or
institutionally unavailable.

**walkerton:** "the system cannot detect when the trusted actor is also the deceiving actor."
Category D: notification architecture that assumes good faith from the actor in the reporting
chain. Walkerton is the cleanest statement of this Category D variant: the system was well-
designed except for the assumption that the operator would always pass on adverse results.
This assumption is not a gap in the architecture — it is built into the architecture's
premise. The O'Connor Commission's fix (simultaneous laboratory-to-health-officer reporting)
did not fix bad actors; it redesigned the architecture so that the actor's intent became
irrelevant. This is the "actor-independent notification" design principle, now with a clean
case in the corpus.

**Summary of new flag patterns:**

The biafra flag (Category J candidate) is the most structurally distinct addition. If
confirmed in a second case, it would name a pattern with major implications for humanitarian
and advocacy organizations: when the actor's funding model and their analytical conclusions
point in opposite directions, principled pragmatism does not tell you which to act on. The
biafra case says acting on the funding model is the structural default — not because the
actors are weak, but because the organization that would perform the honest analysis is the
same organization whose existence would be threatened by that analysis. The framework can
describe this trap; it cannot escape it.

## 5. Prospective cases grow to six: new pattern in the live 18th camel

Adding isa-mining-code-commons-baseline this week brings the prospective count to six:
1. gerd-nile: Reframe
2. tigris-euphrates: Expand
3. tuvalu: Implement
4. indus-waters: Redesign
5. plastic-treaty: Redesign
6. **isa-mining-code-commons-baseline: Redesign** (added this run)

Three of six prospective cases now carry Redesign pathway. A pattern across all three
(indus, plastic-treaty, isa): the 18th camel is an institutional form that creates a new
process boundary — a "without prejudice" negotiation, a "nationally determined trajectories"
commitment framework, an "independent environmental standards panel" phase-gate. All three
camels work by changing what counts as progress within the negotiation without requiring
parties to concede their substantive positions. This is the prospective format's signature:
in live unresolved cases, the camel tends to be a process architecture innovation, not a
substantive solution.

The isa case adds a new flag type to the prospective corpus: "the commons principle names
who owns but not who speaks." Previous prospective flags have been about political will,
sovereignty claims, and epistemic preconditions. This is the first flag about representation
itself as a structural absence — not "one party won't cooperate" but "the party that should
be at the table doesn't exist yet." This may be the corpus's first case where the resolution
requires a principle not yet invented: how to represent future generations in a consensus-
based international governance body. Worth watching across future prospective cases.
