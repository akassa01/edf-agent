## 1. The doing-crowded-out correction: 4 cases in 3 runs

The 2026-08-17 digest noted that doing was the least-covered crowded-out mode (16 of 54 cases,
30%) and that "the quota mechanism will produce the correction if the under-filled cells happen
to contain doing-crowded-out cases." That correction arrived faster than expected.

In weeks 25–27, four of the six new cases crowded out doing:
- haiti-earthquake (community/humanist): humanitarian speed crowded out coordination and
  accountability
- alice-stewart (individual/scientist): doing — clinical x-ray practice — crowded out the
  evidence-gathering that would have revealed harm
- brower-glen-canyon (individual/humanist): the urgency of saving Dinosaur National Monument
  crowded out investigation of what would be sacrificed in its place
- elk-river (community/scientist): restoring water delivery crowded out the thinking required
  to understand what "safe" meant

This is the most concentrated doing-crowded-out run in the corpus (4 of 6 = 67%, vs. the
baseline of 29% over the full corpus). It moves doing from 16 to 20 — cutting the gap behind
thinking and feeling from 4 to 2 — before this run adds thinking and feeling back. After this
run, the distribution is thinking=22, feeling=22, doing=20.

What caused the surge? Three structural factors:

First, two of the four (alice-stewart, elk-river) are cases where an actor with genuine
scientific authority leaned on doing — clinical practice, operational restoration — at the
expense of the gathering-and-refining mode that would have challenged that practice. These
are scientist-archetype cases where the scientist's own doing-mode (running the experiment,
restoring the system) crowds out the thinking that would audit the experiment's assumptions.

Second, brower and haiti are humanist-archetype cases where moral urgency — save the canyon,
feed the earthquake victims — produces doing-mode responses that foreclose the coordination
and reconnaissance the situations required. This is a new structural pattern: the humanist
driven to act immediately by felt urgency, where the feeling-mode's insistence on response
crowds out the doing-mode's patient coordination. This is the reverse of the more common
pattern (feeling crowded out by doing) — here doing is the crowded-out mode even in humanist-
archetype cases, because the actor's urgency collapses the process discipline that makes
doing reliable.

**Implication for the corpus:** doing-crowded-out cases may cluster in two distinct subtypes:
(a) cases where an expert's practice forecloses auditing of that practice (alice-stewart,
elk-river; and by extension challenger's Boisjoly, walkerton's koebel), and (b) cases where
moral urgency produces hasty action before the situation is understood (haiti, brower; and by
extension hawaii-false-alert's speed imperative). These subtypes share the same formal
category (doing crowded out) but have different structural remedies: subtype (a) calls for
second-opinion review architecture; subtype (b) calls for urgency-management protocols.

## 2. Redesign reaches 30 — and the pathway split is now pronounced

At the 2026-08-17 digest (54 cases), the Redesign/Reframe split was 24/20. After this run:

| Pathway | Count | % of corpus |
|---|---|---|
| Redesign | 30 | 47% |
| Reframe | 22 | 34% |
| Expand | 4 | 6% |
| Implement | 2 | 3% |

Redesign now accounts for nearly half the corpus and grows at 1.5× the rate of Reframe over
the last eight cases (6 vs. 2). This is not driven by quota selection — the quota selects for
scale and archetype, not pathway. The Redesign dominance is emerging from the available cases.

The 2026-08-17 digest articulated the structural difference: Redesign cases feature a system
whose architecture generates the wrong output; Reframe cases feature actors answering the
wrong question coherently. Six new cases confirm this distinction:

Redesign additions (drc-ebola, haiti, srilanka, boston-molasses, mekong-delta): In every
case, the architectural problem is identifiable: the vaccination delivery system had no
community-trust layer; the humanitarian response system had no pre-agreed coordination
protocol; the organic-farming mandate had no transitional buffer; Jell's contract process had
no independent certification clause; the delta's adaptation budget has no zoned strategy line
item. In each case, the 18th camel is an institutional form that changes the process sequence.

Reframe additions (alice-stewart, brower, elk-river): In all three, the actors are answering
a coherent but wrong question: Is this x-ray safe? Can we save Dinosaur? Is the water safe to
drink? The 18th camel in each case is a question-substitution: What is the cumulative exposure
threshold we would need to study? What are we trading this canyon for? What should "safe"
mean when the screening level is also a public promise?

The distinction now holds in 53 of 64 cases (83%). The 11 exceptions are cases that straddle
both pathways, which is consistent with this being a valid but not exhaustive taxonomy.

A structural prediction: the ratio may reflect the corpus's historical vs. recent case
distribution. Historical cases (pre-1990) are more likely to be Reframe (the actors were
answering the question their era defined); recent and prospective cases are more likely to be
Redesign (the architecture failures are more visible in retrospect, and the prospective format
encourages institutional prescriptions). Testing this against the data: of the 22 Reframe
cases, 14 are historical, 8 recent. Of the 30 Redesign cases, 17 are historical, 13 recent.
The ratio holds across eras, which weakens the historical-era hypothesis.

## 3. Seven prospective cases — and the mekong-delta completes the zoned-strategy pattern

The prospective corpus now stands at seven:
1. gerd-nile: Reframe
2. tigris-euphrates: Expand
3. tuvalu: Implement
4. indus-waters: Redesign
5. plastic-treaty: Redesign
6. isa-mining-code-commons-baseline: Redesign
7. **mekong-delta-managed-retreat: Redesign** (this run)

The 2026-08-17 digest identified a pattern across the Redesign prospective cases (indus,
plastic-treaty, isa): the 18th camel is consistently a process architecture innovation that
changes what counts as progress without requiring parties to concede substantive positions.

mekong-delta confirms this pattern but adds a structural feature not in the previous three:
the 18th camel is explicitly *spatial* rather than procedural. The zoned adaptive strategy
(hold zones / transition zones / restoration zones) is a territorial redesign — it does not
change what is being negotiated but where different things are negotiated. This is distinct
from the indus "without prejudice" mechanism (which changes the legitimacy of partial
agreements) and the plastic-treaty "nationally determined trajectories" (which changes the
unit of commitment).

Provisional taxonomy of prospective 18th camels:
- **Procedural camels** (change the rules of the game): indus-waters, plastic-treaty, isa
- **Spatial camels** (change which geography gets which rule): mekong-delta
- **Temporal camels** (change when the commitment kicks in or expires): tuvalu
- **Conceptual camels** (change the definition of the problem): gerd-nile, tigris-euphrates

This is a hypothesis with small-n support. Worth watching in future prospective cases.

## 4. New flag patterns: three related variants around resource timing

Since the last digest, three flags share a structural logic that is distinct from previous
flag categories:

**drc-ebola flag:** "legitimacy reconstruction is slower than outbreaks allow, and requires
investment between outbreaks." The required resource (community trust with health workers)
must be built before the crisis. It cannot be built in the weeks after the outbreak starts.

**haiti-earthquake flag:** "donor community willing to invest in prevention rather than
response" — the required resource (pre-agreed coordination framework) must be funded before
the disaster. It is never funded before the disaster.

**drc-ebola and haiti together:** both name a timing gap between when investment would be
effective and when it actually occurs. This is a structural gap, not a political failure: the
political salience of the need (outbreak, earthquake) arrives after the optimal investment
window has closed. Proposing this as a pattern: **the pre-crisis investment paradox**. The
intervention that would work costs least before the crisis and has the least political support
before the crisis. The intervention that is funded after the crisis costs more and works less
well. No principled-pragmatist framework tells you how to secure pre-crisis investment for a
crisis that hasn't happened yet and may not happen before the next election.

This is structurally different from the biafra "constitutive-incentive lock" (Category J):
in biafra, the organization's funding was contingent on performing the wrong action; in
haiti and drc-ebola, the organization's funding would theoretically allow the right action
but arrives in the wrong sequence. The biafra lock is constitutive; the haiti/drc-ebola lock
is temporal. Proposing this as **Category T: temporal-investment lock** — the intervention
that would work requires investment before political salience creates the funding, so it is
structurally underfunded at the moment it could be effective.

**elk-river flag:** "a screening threshold is a scientific object and a public speech act
at the same time." A number from a calculation becomes a promise when announced. The promise
lives in community memory independent of the number's confidence interval. No framework tells
you how to prevent operational pressure from collapsing the tiered advisory structure back
into a single number.

This is the corpus's first flag about scientific communication as a category of irreversibility.
Once a threshold number is announced under crisis conditions, retracting or qualifying it is
experienced as betrayal — the promise cannot be unsaid, even if the evidence basis for it
changes. This is different from all previous flags: not a governance structure failure, not
an incentive lock, not a temporal gap — it is a speech-act failure: the gap between what the
scientist means (a calculated concentration with known uncertainty) and what the public hears
(a guarantee). Proposing as **Category S: the number-as-promise problem** — scientific
communication in crisis conditions creates epistemic commitments that bind more tightly than
the evidence supports.

**Tentative flag taxonomy update (proposed for Prof. Islam's review):**
- A: actor's own limits (ego, Solomon Paradox, cannot-notice-from-inside)
- B: tool insufficiency
- C: [vacant — was proposed in early runs but never filled]
- D: institutional architecture failure
- E: diagnostic-prescriptive gap (framework diagnoses correctly; prescription unavailable)
- J: constitutive-incentive lock (biafra)
- T: temporal-investment lock (haiti, drc-ebola) — new
- S: number-as-promise (elk-river) — new
- [unlabeled: brower's epistemic-constituency gap — advocacy cannot represent what its
  constituency has not experienced; relates to J but distinct enough to merit naming]

## 5. The individual/engineer cell completes to 5 — and a new pattern emerges

Boston-molasses adds the fifth case to the individual/engineer cell (hyatt, citicorp,
tenerife, slotin, boston-molasses):

| Case | Crowded-out mode | What was foreclosed | Flag category |
|---|---|---|---|
| hyatt-regency (1981) | thinking | load calculation on a field design change | D: institutional review gap |
| citicorp (1978) | thinking | disclosure vs. concealment of structural flaw | D: expert authority requires social confirmation |
| tenerife (1977) | thinking | verification before takeoff roll | A: competence+confidence produces unchecked speed |
| slotin (1946) | thinking | separation of designer and safety-checker | D: expert authority and accountability cannot cohabit |
| boston-molasses (1919) | thinking | independent structural review before first fill | D: accountability concentrates where competence is absent |

All five crowd out thinking. All five are retrospective. Four of five carry Category D flags.
The pattern is now highly stable: individual/engineer cases in this corpus consistently feature
an actor whose doing-mode (build it, approve it, take off, assemble it) forecloses the
reflective step (review, disclose, verify, separate) that would have changed the outcome.

Boston-molasses introduces a variant not seen in the previous four: Jell was not a technical
actor at all. The previous four all feature individuals with genuine engineering competence
(LeMessurier was a structural engineer; Van Zanten was an experienced pilot; Slotin was a
nuclear physicist; the Hyatt's Jack Gillum was a structural engineer). Jell was a treasurer.
His doing-mode was commercial execution, not engineering execution. This produces a new
failure pattern in the cell: the non-technical agent with formal accountability who lacks
the competence to identify what the doing-mode is skipping. The previous four cases are about
expert actors who failed to step outside their expertise; boston-molasses is about a non-expert
actor who was never inside it.

This is the **Category D-prime variant**: not "the expert's authority foreclosed review" but
"the accountable actor's incompetence made the review invisible." Both produce the same
structural outcome (liability concentrates where it cannot do work), but they call for
different interventions: D requires separating expertise from accountability; D-prime requires
requiring independent expertise when the accountable actor lacks it.

## 6. The alice-stewart / elk-river pair: a new template for scientist-archetype/doing cases

The alice-stewart and elk-river cases are the first two scientist-archetype cases where the
crowded-out mode is doing rather than feeling. (Previous scientist-archetype cases crowded out
either thinking [pauling, midgley] or feeling [marshall, walkerton, slotin-as-scientist]).

The pattern in both: a scientific actor makes a determination (x-rays at this level are safe;
MCHM at this level is safe) that is then institutionalized — into clinical practice, into a
public announcement — before the evidence base has fully matured. The doing-mode (practice,
deliver, restore) absorbs the determination and makes it hard to revisit. The crowded-out mode
is doing in the sense of *iterative, revisable doing* — the adaptive experiment, the next
study, the tiered announcement with explicit uncertainty. What is foreclosed is not action
but revisable action.

This is a variant of the urgency-actionability trap at the level of scientific governance:
the scientist's determination creates institutional momentum that outruns the scientist's
ability to revise it. The 18th camel in both cases (alice-stewart: independent review
pathway with precautionary interim guidelines; elk-river: tiered advisory with explicit
uncertainty communication) both work by building revisability into the institutionalization
step — ensuring that the number and the practice do not become fused before the evidence
warrants it.

**For the book:** these two cases together articulate a template worth naming. The Scientist
who communicates a determination under pressure creates an institutional path-dependency. The
humanist and engineer in the room hear the determination differently — as a permission to act
(the engineer) and as a commitment to people (the humanist) — and both readings make revision
feel like betrayal. The scientist's failure to communicate their own uncertainty is therefore
not merely an epistemic failure; it is an institutional act with downstream engineering and
humanist consequences.
