## 1. Data confirms the theoretical core: dominant archetypes displace their opposite mode

The corpus now has sufficient coverage to test EDF's central theoretical prediction — that
each dominant archetype displaces its own opposite mode. The data:

| Dominant archetype | Crowded-out feeling | Crowded-out doing | Crowded-out thinking |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scientist (8 cases)** | **6** (mcnamara, yom-kippur, capetown, chernobyl, montreal, marshall) | 2 (challenger, zambia) | 0 |
| **Humanist (7 cases)** | 0 | **5** (emma-daniel, gerd-nile, who-covid, silverman, rana-plaza) | 2 (raj-solomon, nauru) |
| **Engineer (9 cases)** | 3 (hawaii, grenfell, kursk) | 0 | **6** (inderlok, aral-sea, hyatt-regency, citicorp, tenerife, tigris-euphrates) |

The diagonal dominates: scientists displace feeling in 6 of 8 cases; humanists displace doing
in 5 of 7; engineers displace thinking in 6 of 9. The off-diagonal exceptions (challenger
scientist/doing, zambia scientist/doing; raj-solomon humanist/thinking, nauru humanist/thinking;
hawaii engineer/feeling, grenfell engineer/feeling, kursk engineer/feeling) are structurally
interesting — they are cases where the analyst's dominant mode displaces an adjacent mode
rather than its opposite, suggesting a spectrum rather than a binary. But the diagonal
pattern is strong enough that the framework's core prediction is substantiated by the data,
not just asserted by the theory.

**For Prof. Islam:** This is the corpus's strongest quantitative finding. Scientist-dominant
decision environments reliably crowd out the felt dimension of the problem. Humanist-dominant
environments reliably crowd out action. Engineer-dominant environments reliably crowd out
independent diagnosis of the problem space. This is not a pattern the seeds were designed to
produce; it emerged as cases accumulated.

## 2. Decision pathway polarization: Scientist/Humanist → Reframe; Engineer → Redesign

**Current distribution (24 cases):**

| Pathway | Count | Cases |
|---|---|---|
| **Redesign** | 12 | hawaii, inderlok, capetown, grenfell, aral-sea, yom-kippur, hyatt-regency, silverman, citicorp, rana-plaza, tenerife, chernobyl |
| **Reframe** | 11 | mcnamara, challenger, emma-daniel, raj-solomon, gerd-nile, who-covid, nauru, zambia, kursk, montreal, marshall |
| **Expand** | 1 | tigris-euphrates |
| **Implement** | 0 | — |

The most striking finding is not the Reframe/Redesign near-tie — it is the breakdown by
dominant archetype:

- **Scientist dominant (8 cases):** Reframe 5, Redesign 3. Scientists, already in
  analysis mode, face problems where the question is wrong (Reframe) rather than problems
  where the system is wrong (Redesign).
- **Humanist dominant (7 cases):** Reframe 5, Redesign 2. Same logic: humanists who
  crowd out doing tend to need a different question more than a better mechanism.
- **Engineer dominant (9 cases):** Redesign 7, Reframe 1, Expand 1. Engineers who crowd
  out thinking tend to face situations where the right move is to build a better system,
  not to ask a better question. The corpus suggests that engineering-dominated
  decision environments produce problems with clearer system-level interventions, even
  when the diagnosis is wrong.

The **first Expand case** (tigris-euphrates-data-compact) is structurally important. Expand
is the pathway when a working solution already exists at smaller scale and the move is to
apply it to a larger population or scope. The Iraq-Syria bilateral MoU (2025) is the working
solution; Syria's proposal to Turkey is the expansion. This pathway was absent in the corpus
for the first 23 cases. Its entry marks a new type of case: the problem is not conceptual
(Reframe) or mechanical (Redesign) — it is political (Expand requires convincing a reluctant
actor to join something already working).

**Implement (0 cases)** remains absent. This likely reflects selection bias: the corpus
selects for cases where the right move was unclear, ambiguous, or structurally blocked.
Implement cases — where the move is simply to execute an existing plan more faithfully —
may be less pedagogically distinctive. Prof. Islam should decide whether the book needs
implementation failure cases or whether the four-pathway model is better illustrated by
the three that are represented.

## 4. Flag taxonomy: five categories of where EDF stops

The 24 flags now suggest a stable taxonomy of principled pragmatism's limits. They are not
a random collection of caveats — they cluster into five categories, each describing a
different level at which the framework's reach ends.

**Category A — The actor's own limits (5 flags):**
Ego cannot be reasoned with (mcnamara); raw organizational power overrides correct analysis
(challenger); trust, once lost, cannot be rebuilt on demand (hawaii); self-awareness does
not grant immunity from the bias it names (raj-solomon); institutional position is a
prerequisite for the 18th camel, and the framework cannot grant it (marshall).

**Category B — The tool's limits (4 flags):**
Actionable space ≠ guaranteed outcome (emma-daniel); cost distribution is identified but
who pays for the 18th camel is not resolved (inderlok); professional liability systems
designed to assign blame after failure do not have natural incentives to prevent failure
before it (hyatt-regency); voluntary corporate social responsibility operates at the speed
of reputational damage, not the speed of structural reform (rana-plaza).

**Category C — Community and political limits (5 flags):**
Governments may need the conflict to function (gerd-nile); community-level public health
intervention requires community-level consent to succeed (silverman); sovereignty can be
traded away by its own holders (nauru); community voice is structurally excluded from
technical decision systems that are supposed to protect it (grenfell); data-correct
governance can be structurally exclusionary when communities are treated as uniform
behavioral units (capetown).

**Category D — Institutional architecture limits (4 flags):**
An institution whose authority is member-state-derived cannot act against its principals
at the speed the problem requires (who-covid); when institution and principals coincide,
the institution cannot reform itself from within (aral-sea); state sovereignty over civilian
nuclear information is structurally incompatible with nuclear safety being a transboundary
problem (chernobyl); a model that becomes doctrine stops being a model — it becomes a
belief system that can no longer be falsified (yom-kippur).

**Category E — The diagnostic-prescriptive gap (6 flags):**
The framework can advocate; it cannot compel when authoritarian pride is factored into
acceptable loss (kursk); scientific consensus establishes what must change; political
economy governs when (montreal); the pathway runs through the political constraint,
not around it — and whether the political constraint can be moved is not guaranteed
(zambia); the framework finds the pathway but cannot grant you the position from which
to walk it (marshall — also fits Category A); technical compliance can be structurally
indifferent to the knowledge of those it is designed to protect (grenfell — also fits
Category C); the engineering diplomat can build the diagnostic instrument but cannot
compel the fever to break (tigris-euphrates).

The diagnostic-prescriptive gap (Category E) is the corpus's newest and most important
emergent pattern. Eight of the last eleven cases have flags that describe some version
of the same limitation: the framework produces a correct diagnosis and a viable pathway,
but the pathway requires something the framework cannot supply — political will, institutional
position, community consent, or the capacity to coerce. This is not a weakness of the
framework; it is the map of the adjacent problem space. EDF reaches the boundary where
technical analysis meets non-technical constraints. The book's final chapter may be about
that boundary.

**For Prof. Islam:** The five-category taxonomy is book material. "Where EDF Stops" is not
a list of apologies — it is a structured account of what principled pragmatism can and
cannot reach, and why. The progression from Category A (the actor) to Category E (the
diagnostic-prescriptive gap) maps an increasing scale of structural constraint.

## 5. Cross-case regularity: 18th camel as category shift holds across all 24 cases

The July 11 digest identified this pattern in the first 8 cases. It holds across all 24.
In every case where the 18th camel exists and is defended, it is a move to a different
frame — a category shift, not an optimization within the existing frame:

- Citicorp: from "fix the structural flaw in secret" vs. "disclose publicly" to "managed
  multi-party disclosure with engineers, lawyers, and Buildings Department simultaneously"
- Rana Plaza: from "audit factories" vs. "boycott brands" to "legally enforceable
  inspection authority with domestic standing, not brand discretion"
- Nauru: from "accept the trust fund's terms" vs. "reject and remain poor" to "reframe
  phosphate extraction as an ICJ case establishing a legal precedent for colonial resource
  exploitation"
- Tenerife: from "KLM captain complies with ATC" vs. "ATC prevents the takeoff" to
  "redesign the communication protocol so clearance confirmation is unambiguous and
  copilot challenge is structurally enabled"
- Chernobyl: from "Soviet Union discloses vs. conceals" to "design an international
  early-warning mechanism so disclosure is not a sovereign choice but an automatic
  transboundary obligation"
- Marshall: from "prove more" vs. "give up" to "invite the skeptics to co-produce the
  proof, so the finding belongs to their evidence base"
- Tigris-Euphrates: from "negotiate water rights" vs. "accept status quo" to "share
  hydrological data without conceding legal position — not about who gets what, but
  about what is actually flowing"

The pattern: the 18th camel is visible only from outside the frame the two stated options
share. When both stated options argue about the same thing (who is right, who gets what,
whether to act), the 18th camel changes what is being argued about (how to verify, how to
share authority, how to build the diagnostic instrument). This is the corpus's most
teachable and most consistent finding.

## 6. Prospective cases: growing as a sub-corpus

The corpus now has two prospective cases: gerd-nile (2026-07-11) and tigris-euphrates
(2026-07-24). Both are international scale. Both have 90-day commitment structures rather
than retrospective counterfactuals.

A pattern in the prospective cases: the "where is X" is a time window rather than a
single moment, and the synthesis block is a live recommendation under uncertainty rather
than a lesson drawn from failure. This changes the pedagogical function — the case
becomes advocacy rather than autopsy. Whether this is a feature or a limitation depends
on whether Prof. Islam wants EDF to be used as an analytical tool (retrospective cases)
or a decision support tool (prospective cases). The corpus currently supports both, with
the retrospective cases outnumbering prospective 22:2.

The prospective cases also test the flag's function. The tigris-euphrates flag —
"the engineering diplomat can build the diagnostic instrument; cannot compel the fever
to break" — is written in live uncertainty, not the certainty of hindsight. This is
harder to anchor. The gerd-nile flag — "political will may be absent not because leaders
don't see the solution but because the conflict is a resource" — was similarly written
prospectively. Both flags function; the diagnostic-prescriptive gap flags may be inherently
more natural in prospective cases, where the limitation is still the operative constraint.

## 8. Historical vs. recent split: now roughly balanced

The corpus through week_index 9: historical 13, recent 11. This is a meaningful shift
from the seed corpus, which was 2 historical (mcnamara, challenger) and 4 recent. As
historical cases have accumulated, the balance has regularized.

Pedagogical implication: the book can draw examples from both registers without
appearing to rely exclusively on cases distant enough to have settled into consensus.
The recent cases (gerd-nile, who-covid, capetown, grenfell, rana-plaza, zambia,
tigris-euphrates, and more) allow readers to disagree with the analysis on grounds of
current events — which is pedagogically preferable to cases so historical that the
lesson has already been canonized.
