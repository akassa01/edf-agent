**Humanist/doing reaches 9 cases — the single largest diagonal cell.** The two new entries

systematic failure mode of technical organizations under time and budget pressure.

**For Prof. Islam:** The diagonal pattern is the corpus's most robust finding at 38 cases. The

## 2. Decision pathway distribution: Redesign extends its lead; Reframe grows; Expand reaches three

**Updated distribution (38 cases):**

| Pathway | Count | New this digest | Cases added |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Redesign** | 19 | +3 (gandhi, bangladesh, pauling) | Largest pathway; all three new additions are individual or international scale |
| **Reframe** | 15 | +2 (love-canal, lapindo) | Both new cases decouple the humanitarian question from the liability/legal question |
| **Expand** | 3 | +1 (mbeki) | Now has three entries supporting comparison |
| **Implement** | 1 | — | Tuvalu; still the only prospective-Implement case |

**Redesign (19) extends its lead.** The three new Redesign cases share an unusual property:
in all three, the binding constraint is the actor's own internal framework. Gandhi's ahimsa as
indivisible made the regional option inadmissible before it was examined. Pauling's certainty
that retrospective data was already sufficient proof prevented engagement with a cooperative
trial structure. Bangladesh's engineer-dominant program had no protocol for discovering
hazards outside its specified test panel. In each case, the Redesign move requires the actor
to modify a framework they cannot see as a framework, because it functions as a definition
of the problem space. This is distinct from earlier Redesign cases (grenfell, tenerife,
chernobyl) where the system to be redesigned is an external architecture, not the actor's
own conceptual apparatus.

**Reframe (15) gains two structurally parallel entries.** Love Canal and Lapindo, written in
the same week, produce camels with the same architecture: decouple the humanitarian question
(do these families need help now?) from the legal question (who caused this and who pays?)
by establishing parallel proceedings running at different speeds. The structural similarity
across cases separated by 28 years, two continents, and different legal systems strengthens
the pattern's generalizability — the decoupling logic is not context-specific. It is a
category shift that the standard binary (accept/reject the claim) structurally prevents.

**Expand now has three entries.** The comparison is instructive. Tigris-Euphrates: extend a
bilateral data-sharing compact to a third sovereign actor. Nubian-aswan: extend the UNESCO
"protected heritage" concept from monuments to living peoples. Mbeki: extend the scope of
existing willing-province health programs to create a functioning pilot before national
rollout. The first two expand an institutional mechanism to a broader scope. The third
expands a political permission — from "some provinces may try" to "some provinces are
authorized and supported." All three share the structure: an existing working principle or
mechanism is applied to a population or scope it was not designed for.

## 4. Flag taxonomy update: Category D doubles; Category A grows; a new Category G candidate

**Updated counts (38 cases):**

| Category | Name | Previous | New cases | Count |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Actor's own limits | 6 | gandhi, pauling | 8 |
| B | Tool's limits | 6 | — | 6 |
| C | Community and political limits | 5 | mbeki | 6 |
| D | Institutional architecture limits | 6 | love-canal, lapindo | 8 |
| E | Diagnostic-prescriptive gap | 8 | — | 8 |
| F (candidate) | Feedback destruction | 1 | — | 1 |
| G (candidate) | Unknown-unknown epistemological limits | 0 | bangladesh | 1 |

**Category A (Actor's own limits) grows to 8 cases** with two structurally different new
additions. Pauling's flag ("there is no mechanism that imposes clinical-domain humility on an
authority whose credibility was earned elsewhere") is a clean Category A: the limit is the
actor's inability to recognize that domain expertise does not transfer. Gandhi's flag
("A leader whose authority derives from refusing to calculate cannot be reached by a framework
that asks him to calculate differently") is a harder case: the limit is not incapacity but
constitution. Gandhi's refusal to calculate was not an error in his reasoning; it was the
source of his moral authority. The framework cannot reach him not because he fails to reason
but because reasoning-about-options is itself incompatible with the role his authority
requires him to play. This is a new structural variant within Category A: the actor's limit
is not a cognitive failure but an identity constraint.

**Category D (Institutional architecture limits) grows to 8 cases.** Love Canal adds a
statutory variant: the evidentiary standard for protective government action and the standard
for federal expenditure liability were, in 1978, the same standard set by the same
institution — and no law gave an independent scientific body authority to compel federal
relocation without that statutory coupling. Lapindo adds a jurisdictional variant: peer-
reviewed scientific consensus has no enforcement mechanism within a domestic legal system
subject to political capture, because such enforcement requires treaty law and no sovereign
state has signed a treaty constraining its own courts' authority over domestic corporate
liability. Both are institutional gaps in the specific sense: the tools exist (independent
panels, escrow funds, arbitration protocols), but no institution has the legal authority to
make the tools binding on the parties who need to comply.

**Category D (now tied with A for largest) has a cross-case pattern**: all eight cases share
a structure in which engineering diplomacy can design the right tool but cannot supply the
institutional authority to make it stick. The tool requires a legal mandate that does not yet
exist. In five of the eight cases (nubian-aswan, tuvalu, love-canal, lapindo, and gerd-nile),
the gap is visible in advance — the institutional mechanism EDF would recommend is
identifiable before the crisis; it simply has not been built. This is a lesson about the
time-scale of institutional development relative to the time-scale of disasters.

**Bangladesh (flag: "Principled pragmatism has no algorithm for discovering unknown unknowns")
may warrant Category G.** The distinction from existing categories: Category E describes the
gap between knowing the answer and being able to act on it. Bangladesh describes a prior gap:
the inability to form the question before the harm has accumulated. The program's testing
protocol was not wrong about what it tested; it was blind to what it did not test. This is an
epistemological limit that survives every institutional reform: redesigning the protocol
requires knowing what to add, which requires having discovered what was missing, which
requires being harmed by its absence. The causation runs in a circle. Category E flags
describe the diagnostic-prescriptive gap. Category G (candidate) would describe the
diagnostic-formulation gap — the limit that appears before diagnosis is possible.

**For Prof. Islam:** If Category G is accepted, it reframes what kind of book Engineering
Diplomacy is. Categories A-F describe limits on action given correct diagnosis. Category G
describes a limit that precedes diagnosis — a constraint on what any learning system can know
about its own blind spots before those blind spots produce harm. One case does not constitute
a category. It may be a boundary case of B (tool's limits — the protocol's limit is that it
only tests what it specifies) or it may be genuinely prior. The decision matters for the
chapter structure.

## 5. New cross-case pattern: the internally coherent humanist foreclosure

The two new humanist/doing cases (gandhi, mbeki) introduce a pattern absent from the prior
seven humanist/doing cases: the situation where the actor's humanist reasoning is internally
consistent, explicitly articulated, and produces a dead-end for the 18th camel not through
bad reasoning but through the premises the reasoning accepts.

Previous humanist/doing cases — emma-daniel through nubian-aswan — all share the structure:
the humanist actor has the right values but the wrong institutional tool. The Nile Basin
negotiators know that riparian communities matter but have no legal instrument. The WHO
health officials know that transparency matters but have no reporting mandate. The 18th camel
in each case supplies the missing instrument.

Gandhi and Mbeki are different. Gandhi knew the regional option existed and declined it
because his conception of ahimsa made geographic exceptions an internal contradiction. Mbeki
knew that Western Cape and Gauteng were willing to proceed and declined authorization because
his conception of postcolonial epistemic sovereignty made pharmaceutical-data acceptance an
ideological concession. The 18th camel fails not because the actor lacks the tool but because
the actor cannot accept the camel without abandoning the premises that constitute their
authority.

The implication for pedagogy: EDF can design the camel, but the camel requires the actor to
accept that their principle has a geographic expression (gandhi) or an epistemic scope limit
(mbeki). When the principle is indivisible-by-design, the camel is illegible to the person
who would walk it. The intervention point is not the actor's reasoning but the actor's
premises — which means the intervention point is someone with standing inside the actor's own
value framework who can reframe the premise, not the conclusion.

Both cases confirm: the effective point of intervention is a trusted insider who speaks the
actor's moral language and can say "your principle permits this exception" or "your question
separates from their question." In both cases, that person did not appear, or appeared too
late. The framework can name the role. It cannot supply the person.

## 6. 18th camel cross-case pattern: now 38 cases, same structural regularity

Six new camels, all category shifts:

- **Gandhi**: from "suspend the national movement" vs. "don't suspend" to regional
  accountability with geographic bounding and succession mandate — changes the
  *scope* of the principle's expression from national to provincial
- **Mbeki**: from "block Nevirapine nationally" vs. "implement nationally" to provincial
  parallel pilot with separated terms of reference — changes the *framing* of one question
  (pharmaceutical trust) from being the same as another (treatment safety)
- **Bangladesh**: from "rapid tubewell rollout" vs. "delay for comprehensive testing" to
  geological-zone phased deployment with multi-contaminant baseline — changes the *model*
  of safety from single-hazard to multi-hazard
- **Pauling**: from "publish as proof" vs. "withhold until RCT" to submit as pilot evidence
  with jointly designed NCI trial — changes the *epistemic status* of the data from claim
  to hypothesis
- **Love Canal / Lapindo**: (both) from "accept/reject the community's evidence as sufficient
  for full action" to decouple the humanitarian proceeding from the liability proceeding,
  running at different speeds — changes the *temporal structure* of justice from sequential
  (liability first, then aid) to parallel (aid now, liability later)

The 38-case regularity holds: the camel is never a compromise between the two options; it
is a category shift that changes what is being contested. Bangladesh and pauling both add a
new sub-pattern: the camel changes the *epistemic register* of the dispute (from proof to
hypothesis; from single-hazard to multi-hazard model). Love Canal and Lapindo are the first
two camels in the corpus with an identical structural solution across independent cases —
the parallel-proceedings decoupling. Their co-appearance in week 16 is a methodological
artifact, but the structural parallel is real and generalizable.
