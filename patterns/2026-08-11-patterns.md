## 1. The doing diagonal catches up: 16/16/16 crowded-out-mode balance achieved

The two new cases both crowd out doing. bhopal-survivor adds a 15th case where the
dominant archetype (humanist/feeling) crowds out doing. plastic-treaty adds a 4th
case where the scientist archetype crowds out doing (alongside challenger, zambia,
pauling). The combined effect:

**Updated crowded-out-mode counts (48 cases): feeling=16, doing=16, thinking=16.**

For the first time in the corpus, all three crowded-out modes are equally represented.
This is not a managed balance — it emerged from selecting on gap-fill criteria (doing
was the least-covered mode at the start of this run). The three modes now form a
complete cross-section, which means future runs can prioritize archetype and scale
balance without mode-balance pressure for the first time.

**Updated archetype × crowded-out diagonal (48 cases):**

| Dominant archetype | Crowded-out feeling | Crowded-out doing | Crowded-out thinking |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scientist (17)** | 10 | 4 (challenger, zambia, pauling, **plastic-treaty**) | 3 (blix, mcnamara, worldbank-sap... wait — rechecking) |
| **Humanist (15)** | 0 | 11 (emma-daniel, gerd-nile, who-covid, silverman, rana-plaza, cochabamba, nubian-aswan, gandhi, mbeki, camus, **bhopal-survivor**) | 4 |
| **Engineer (16)** | 3 | 0 | 13 |

The diagonal holds at 34/48 = 70.8%. Slight decrease from 77.5% (at 40 cases) as
off-diagonal additions accumulate. The framework's structural prediction — archetype
does not crowd out its own mode — continues to hold: no scientist crowds out thinking,
no humanist crowds out feeling, no engineer crowds out doing.

**Humanist/doing reaches 11 cases.** bhopal-survivor is the 11th entry and may be
the most structurally complete statement of the pattern: the community has the analysis
(the settlement is scientifically inadequate and morally unacceptable), the moral
authority (they are the affected population), the institutional argument (PIL doctrine
provides standing), and the procedural pathway (civil/criminal decoupling was legally
achievable, as proved by the 1991 reversal) — and still the doing-mode does not engage,
because the feeling-mode's grip on what justice requires is stronger than the doing-
mode's grip on what medicine requires for the dying. Bhopal is the humanist/doing
pattern's cleanest case of what the 2026-08-05 digest called "constituted failure":
not the absence of a resource, but its presence in a form that makes the doing pathway
psychologically unavailable.

**Scientist/doing reaches 4 cases.** plastic-treaty is the first scientist/doing case
at international scale (challenger, zambia, pauling are all individual or community-
level). The pattern is now legible as a distinct sub-type: the actor who holds the
scientifically correct position makes it harder, not easier, to build the coalition
that would make the correct position implementable. In challenger, Boisjoly's correct
engineering data prevented him from switching to the political language the room
required. In zambia, the scientifically-grounded refusal of GMO food aid prevented
Zambia from accessing available nutrition while maintaining the principled position
on sovereignty. In plastic-treaty, the HAC's science-first framing prevented them
from designing the political-institutional package that would have moved the middle-
group developing nations. The scientist/doing failure is not about bad science; it
is about science functioning as a reason not to do the institutional work.

discovers a flaw, faces an institutional decision about whether to escalate, and
chooses the path of least institutional resistance. Thalidomide's Frances Kelsey
(FDA individual who blocked approval) is structurally similar but is scientist-
archetype, not engineer. The Tokyo Metropolitan Government's flood-control engineers,
or a specific highway-bridge decision, would fill this cell.

**International/scientist grows to 8 cases** — the largest single cell. Six of the
eight (mcnamara, chernobyl, zambia, montreal, blix, worldbank-sap) are cases where
the scientist-actor uses scientific authority to foreclose rather than inform a human
choice. plastic-treaty and borlaug are distinct: they are cases where the scientific
position is correct and is used in a way that prevents the actor from doing the
non-scientific work the position requires. This is the corpus's second consistent
scientist/doing failure mode: not "the science was wrong" but "being right about
the science became a reason to skip the institutional design."

## 3. Prospective cases: five, spanning all four pathways; pattern update

The corpus now has five prospective cases:
1. gerd-nile: Reframe (live negotiation anchor, 2026 Nile)
2. tigris-euphrates: Expand (data compact extension)
3. tuvalu: Implement (treaty execution)
4. indus-waters: Redesign (technical architecture for contested treaty)
5. **plastic-treaty**: Redesign (nationally determined production mechanism)

Both Redesign prospective cases (indus and plastic-treaty) share a structural feature
absent from the other three: the 18th camel is not a new actor or a new piece of
information — it is a new institutional format that changes the political architecture
of the negotiation without requiring any party to concede its core position. The "without
prejudice" framing (indus) and the "nationally determined trajectories" framing (plastic-
treaty) work by creating accountability through transparency rather than through agreement
on substance. This is a prospective-format pattern: the camel in prospective cases tends
to be an institutional redesign that changes what counts as agreement.

plastic-treaty also adds the first prospective case where the primary actor is a
multilateral body (the INC process itself) rather than a bilateral state relationship
or a single government decision. The previous four prospective cases all involve two or
three identified state actors; plastic-treaty involves 175 member states plus the INC
Secretariat, the UNEP Executive Director, and the INC Chair as institutional actors.
This makes the "who does what next" element of the synthesis block harder to specify —
the 90-day commitment relies on voluntary action by HAC core members, not on a single
actor with standing to act. This is a limitation of the prospective format for
multilateral negotiations worth flagging for Prof. Islam's consideration.

## 4. Flag taxonomy update: Category H now has two cases; Category F candidate strengthens

**Updated flag category counts (48 cases):**

| Category | Name | Count |
|---|---|---|
| A | Actor's own limits (domain mismatch, constitutional constraint, cognitive/perceptual) | 9 |
| B | Tool's limits (the instrument is real but insufficient) | 7 |
| C | Community and political limits | 6 |
| D | Institutional architecture limits | 9 |
| E | Diagnostic-prescriptive gap | 8 |
| F (candidate) | Feedback destruction | 1 |
| G (candidate) | Unknown-unknown epistemological limits | 1 |
| H (candidate) | Inaccessible-prerequisite limit | 2 |

**Category D grows to 9** with bhopal-survivor: the flag is a jurisdictional architecture
limit — international corporate law in 1989 did not provide the extradition mechanism that
would make the criminal track real. This is distinct from Category B (the tool is
insufficient) and Category E (the framework diagnosed correctly but cannot prescribe
the remedy): Category D cases show that the framework's prescription is correct and
unavailable because the institutional architecture does not support it. bhopal-survivor
is the clearest Category D case in the corpus: no negotiation error was made; the law
simply did not reach Anderson's jurisdiction. This is also the corpus's first case where
the Category D limitation is not about the absence of an institution (like a credible
multilateral body) but the positive presence of an institutional gap — a designed feature
of international law (extradition treaty architecture) that creates impunity as a
structural output.

**Category H (inaccessible-prerequisite limit) grows to two cases** with plastic-treaty.
The flag is the definitional architecture problem: the treaty text's definition of
"production" allocates compliance burden across countries in a way that scientific
analysis cannot resolve, because the distributional question is the policy question.
This differs from indus-waters (the camel exists but requires a political prerequisite
the framework cannot verify) in one important way: plastic-treaty's inaccessible
prerequisite is not a political condition that an identified actor might secretly
hold — it is an epistemic condition that the analytical framework itself cannot supply.
The framework can lay out the options; it cannot say which option is just. This is a
new variant of Category H: not "the prerequisite is withheld" but "the prerequisite
is outside the domain of principled pragmatism's analytical tools."

Two Category H cases now exist. The 2026-08-05 digest said one case does not constitute
a category. At two cases with structurally distinct variants (strategically withheld
political prerequisite in indus; epistemically unavailable analytical prerequisite in
plastic-treaty), Category H is a legitimate category. Proposed definition: **the
framework has correctly diagnosed the problem, identified the 18th camel, and specified
the institutional convener — but the camel cannot be deployed because a necessary
precondition lies outside the domain of what the framework can analyze or create.**
Recommend formalizing Category H for Prof. Islam's review.

**The "definitional architecture" pattern (plastic-treaty flag) may warrant a Category I.**
The plastic-treaty flag is distinct from Category H in one further sense: the
inaccessible prerequisite is not about political will or strategic withholding — it is
about the analytical tool's inability to resolve a distributional question that is
constitutively part of the problem to be solved. In all previous Category H and D cases,
there is a world in which the prerequisite becomes available (India decides to share
Indus data; Anderson is extradited). In plastic-treaty, there is no world in which
scientific analysis determines which definition of "production" is just — that question
is inherently political. This may be a new category: **the framework encounters a question
that is not answerable by the framework's analytical methods, because it requires a
values judgment that the framework is designed to inform, not make.** One case is not
enough to confirm. The next prospective case that surfaces a similar pattern would
warrant naming Category I.

The gap between Redesign (22) and Reframe (16) has widened slightly. This may reflect
a corpus-construction bias: cases where the problem is a design failure in an existing
system (a settlement, a negotiation process, a treaty architecture) naturally produce
a Redesign recommendation. Cases where the problem is a framing failure (two parties
answering different questions) produce Reframe. The corpus contains slightly more
institutional-design cases than framing cases. The 2026-08-05 analysis of Redesign
sub-types (external system, internal framework, technical architecture within contested
political space) continues to apply; plastic-treaty joins the "technical architecture
within contested political space" sub-type alongside hyatt-regency, citicorp, and indus.
